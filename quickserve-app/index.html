Problem

When a fan stops or a pipe bursts, people call whoever a neighbour knows. They can't check skill or price, and many workers never show up.

Families in Tier-2 towns like Kakinada rely on word of mouth and WhatsApp forwards to find electricians and plumbers. Listing sites show phone numbers, but nothing about whether the person is safe to let in, when they'll arrive or what the visit will cost. The result is no-shows, surprise bills and a lot of repeated calling.

This is a concept. Findings below are research assumptions to be validated, and all outcome numbers are design targets.

Research

Interview questions (5–10 households)

  1. The last time you needed an electrician or plumber, how did you find one?
  2. How long did it take before someone actually came?
  3. What went wrong: late, no-show, price, quality?
  4. What would make you trust someone you've never met?
  5. Would you rather know the price before or after the job?
  6. How do you prefer to pay: UPI, cash, card?

Also talk to 3 workers

  • How do they get jobs today?
  • Why do they skip some bookings?
  • What info do they need before visiting?
  • How do they handle extra parts on site?

Expected findings

Trust beats speedPeople would wait an extra hour for someone with a verified ID and real reviews over a faster stranger.
Surprise costs cause the most angerThe visit itself is rarely the issue. The fight is over parts added on site without agreement.
Silence after booking feels like a no-showWithout a message or location, people start calling other workers within 30 minutes.

Insight: trust is built from three things shown up front: a verified face, a price list, and a live update once they're on the way.

Never a dead end

When nobody is free in the urgent window, the app explains why and offers three next steps. The earliest real slot comes first, so Lakshmi isn't left calling around again.

Other edge paths: the worker cancels, so the app re-matches automatically and tells the customer. An extra part costs more than ₹100, so the worker sends a price request in chat and fits the part only after the customer approves it.
